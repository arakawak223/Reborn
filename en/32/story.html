The Comeback Story

The comeback of Mami Sato, told in 5 chapters

01

Origin–2002

The Girl Raised by the Sea Breeze of Kesennuma

Kesennuma City, Miyagi Prefecture. A town facing the rich waters of the Sanriku Coast — this is where Mami grew up. She enrolled at Waseda University and thrived as a member of the cheerleading squad, a bright and energetic student. She loved sports, and moving her body was her reason for being.

In her second year of university, she noticed something wrong with her right leg. The hospital delivered the diagnosis: osteosarcoma — a malignant bone tumor. The reality thrust upon this twenty-year-old girl was unbearably cruel.

To treat the cancer, her right leg was amputated below the knee. From the body that had so naturally run, jumped, and danced, her right leg was taken away. The campus scenes of her university friends laughing together suddenly felt like a world impossibly far away.

02

Despair2002–2011

Phantom Pain and the Tsunami — A Double Despair

What tormented Mami after surgery was phantom limb pain. Her amputated right leg ached as if it were still there. A limb that no longer existed screaming in agony — the absurd cruelty of that suffering ate away at her mind as well. Days confined to her room. Avoiding light, avoiding people, shutting herself away.

Still, Mami stood back up. She fitted a prosthetic leg, discovered the long jump, and began carving out a place in the world of para athletics. But fate struck her down once more.

March 11, 2011. The Great East Japan Earthquake. Mami's hometown of Kesennuma was swallowed by a massive tsunami. Her family home was washed away. Friends and acquaintances lost their lives. First her right leg, and now her hometown. A double despair pressed down upon her with crushing weight.

03

The Void2011–2012

Finding a Reason to Run in the Disaster Zone

After the earthquake, Mami traveled to Kesennuma. Her hometown had been reduced to mountains of rubble. Nothing of the old landscape remained. Yet what she encountered there were people trying to look forward, even in the depths of despair.

"I want to bring smiles to the people in the disaster zone." With that single-minded resolve, Mami began running again. If the sight of her jumping on a prosthetic leg could make even one person smile. If showing that she could run despite losing a leg could become a beacon of hope for those who had lost everything.

"I was saved by sports. That's why I want to give back through sports." This conviction became Mami's new driving force. She discovered the meaning of running not for herself, but for others. The support of her prosthetist, the encouragement of her university friends, the love of her family — carrying the hopes of many on her shoulders, she stood on the track once more.

04

AwakeningSeptember 2013

The Olympic Bid — The Speech That Moved the World

September 7, 2013. The IOC General Session in Buenos Aires. The final presentation to decide the host city for the 2020 Summer Olympics. Mami stood at the podium.

As a para-athlete with a prosthetic leg, and as a survivor of the Great East Japan Earthquake, she addressed the world. She spoke of how "the power of sport" had saved her. Of how the people in the disaster zone had found courage through sports. Of what it would mean to hold the Olympics in Tokyo.

Delivered in fluent English, yet infused with emotion that rose from the very depths of her heart, her speech moved the IOC delegates. The result: Tokyo was selected as the host city for the 2020 Olympics.

"It was her speech that brought the Olympics to Tokyo. That kind of persuasive power belongs only to someone who has overcome despair." A member of the bid committee said those words — and they were no exaggeration. The words of a woman who had lost her right leg, lost her hometown, and still believed in the power of sport had moved the entire world.

05

Rebirth2013–

A Person with Wings in Her Heart

Having become an icon of the Olympic bid, Mami later transitioned to para triathlon, continuing to take on new challenges. She married, had children, and began walking the path of life as a mother.

"Even without a leg, my heart has wings." This statement is her way of life made manifest. She lost her right leg to osteosarcoma, endured phantom limb pain, lost her hometown to a tsunami, and still she kept running. And the sight of her running moved the world and brought the Olympics to Tokyo.

Mami Sato's story is about what lies on the other side of loss — it is a story of what is gained. Because she lost her right leg, there were landscapes she could see that she never would have otherwise. Because she lost her hometown, she felt the warmth of human kindness in ways she never had before. Hidden within a life that seemed to have lost everything were the most precious things of all.

The sea breeze of Kesennuma continues to push against her back to this day.
