The Comeback Story

The comeback of Naoko Takahashi, told in 5 chapters

01

Origin–1999

A Girl Who Loved to Run

Gifu City, Gifu Prefecture. From a young age, Naoko Takahashi simply loved to run. She ran home from school, ran on the way to play with friends——running was as natural to her as breathing.

After graduating from university, she joined a corporate team and there met the mentor who would change her life: coach Yoshio Koide. Famous for his philosophy of "praise to develop," the legendary coach recognized Naoko's bright personality and extraordinary stamina.

Affectionately nicknamed "Q-chan" by the nation, Takahashi blossomed rapidly under Koide's guidance. In 1998 she set a Japanese record at the Nagoya International Women's Marathon, becoming a star of the marathon world overnight.

"Enjoying what you do is the shortest path to success"——her style of running with a smile gave courage to countless other runners.

02

Despair2000–2001

Plantar Fasciitis——Days of Searing Pain with Every Step

The 2000 Sydney Olympics. Naoko Takahashi won the first-ever Olympic gold medal for a Japanese woman in track and field. Her beaming smile as she broke the tape moved the entire nation.

But the price of glory was steep. After the Games, she developed plantar fasciitis. Every time she ran, searing pain shot through the sole of her foot. For a marathon runner, the sole absorbs the full force of every impact. That foundation had broken.

A knee injury compounded the problem. For a woman whose entire life was running, being unable to run was a cruelty beyond imagination. "I loved training so much that I kept ignoring my body's distress signals," she would later reflect. Coach Koide himself admitted, "My job was to reduce her training load"——that is how obsessive a trainer she was.

03

The VoidEarly 2001

The Woman Who Trained Everything "Except Her Soles" to Be the Best in Japan

"While I couldn't run because of plantar fasciitis, I trained every muscle except the soles of my feet to be the best in Japan."

She could not run. Then she would build a body capable of peak performance the moment she could. Takahashi flipped her thinking and threw herself into thousands of daily sit-ups and core exercises. Pool running, cycling, upper-body strength training.

The frustration of not running and the hope of the day she would again. Swaying between the two, she refused to waste a single day.

Coach Koide watched her and said: "Q-chan's bright spirit comes through directly in her running. That's why she's so strong." True to those words, her smile never disappeared even during rehabilitation. She laughed when she wanted to cry, smiled when she was suffering. That resilience sustained her.

04

AwakeningSeptember 2001

The Berlin Marathon——A New World Record

September 30, 2001. The Berlin Marathon. In her comeback race from plantar fasciitis, Naoko Takahashi achieved the unthinkable: she won the race while setting a new women's marathon world record.

2 hours, 19 minutes, 46 seconds. No time a runner recovering from injury should have been capable of. The core strength she had forged during rehabilitation and the fresh leg power stored during her time away from running combined to produce the performance of a lifetime.

"Enjoying what you do is the shortest path to success"——on her face as she crossed the finish line was the same smile as Sydney, or perhaps an even brighter one. Having experienced injury, the joy of simply being able to run had deepened immeasurably.

"She never rests from training, even when injured. My job was to reduce her training load."——Coach Koide's words speak to her staggering passion for practice.

05

Rebirth2008–

The Legacy "Q-chan" Left Japan

Naoko Takahashi retired having left an indelible footprint on the Japanese marathon world. Her Sydney Olympic gold medal, her world record, and above all her gift of conveying the pure joy of running to the entire nation——the magnitude of her contribution is immeasurable.

She received the People's Honor Award. After retirement, she has remained active as a guest runner at marathons and as a television commentator, continuing to share the joy of running. The sight of her running alongside everyday citizens still gives courage to many.

Her story of overcoming plantar fasciitis and returning with a world record stands as proof of "the power of enjoyment." Smile when times are hardest. Look forward when the pain is greatest. Naoko Takahashi's smile is a beacon of hope for every runner who battles through the 42.195 kilometers.

Her mentor, Yoshio Koide, passed away in 2019. But his "praise to develop" philosophy lives on through Q-chan's smile, running onward forever.
