The Comeback Story

The comeback of Daisuke Ikezaki, told in 5 chapters

01

OriginChildhood–2000s

A Fading Body, a Blazing Soul

Born in Hakodate, Hokkaido, Daisuke Ikezaki was diagnosed with Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease in early childhood. A progressive neurological disorder, it gradually robs the extremities of muscle strength and sensation. There is no cure.

As a boy, he threw himself into wheelchair basketball. But as the disease advanced, even the grip strength needed to hold a basketball began to slip away. When he was forced to leave wheelchair basketball behind, it was in the depths of despair that he discovered wheelchair rugby.

Wheelchair rugby is a "combat sport" in which wheelchairs collide with ferocious force. Even if you cannot grip the ball, you can carry it on your lap and play. This encounter with the sport transformed Ikezaki's life.

02

Despair2000s–2010s

A Progressing Disease——The Terror of Vanishing Grip Strength

Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease marched on without mercy. Year by year, his grip grew weaker. The sensation in his feet grew duller. What he could do yesterday, he could not do today.

The most terrifying thing was the certainty that the future held only further decline. There was no hope of recovery. A life in which capabilities only diminished. That terror was heavier than words could express.

Moreover, the violent collisions of wheelchair rugby inflicted severe punishment on his body. Injuries during competition were not uncommon. A body weakened by disease absorbing the brutal impacts of an intense sport. Under normal circumstances, reasons to quit were endless.

Yet Ikezaki declared: "Rugby is a combat sport. The impact that smashes a wheelchair to pieces is proof that I'm alive."

03

The Void2010s

Train What Remains Tenfold

"Even as the disease shuts down more and more of my body, I just have to train what's left ten times harder."

Ikezaki devised his own training methods that adapted to the progression of his condition. If his grip weakened, he would strengthen his wrists and forearms. If his feet lost sensation, he would steer his wheelchair with his core and upper body. He compensated for lost function with what remained——and then surpassed it.

His regimen was staggering. He forged a "steel upper body" capable of withstanding the tackles of wheelchair rugby, and rose to become one of the highest-scoring players in the world.

The support of his family was immeasurable. His wife and children gave him the strength to endure punishing training. "I don't want my kids to see their father give up"——that conviction drove Ikezaki forward.

04

Awakening2016–2023

From Bronze to the Road to Gold

The 2016 Rio Paralympics: a bronze medal. The 2021 Tokyo Paralympics: bronze again. The summit of the world remained just out of reach.

Two bronze medals. The frustration was immeasurable. Yet Ikezaki refused to give up. Even in his mid-forties, he continued to compete at the highest level in the world. Alongside a disease that never stopped advancing.

"His tackle is the heaviest in the world. I have never once seen him use his disease as an excuse."——A teammate's words capture the essence of the man.

His grip continued to weaken. Yet the force of his tackles continued to grow. It sounds like a contradiction, but that was Daisuke Ikezaki. Whatever he lost, he trained twice as hard to reclaim.

05

Rebirth2024

The Long-Awaited Gold in Paris

The 2024 Paris Paralympics. At age 46, Daisuke Ikezaki finally seized the gold medal he had long dreamed of. After bronze in Rio and bronze in Tokyo, he reached the summit on his third attempt.

A man carrying a progressive, incurable disease, losing grip strength and sensation in his feet year by year, yet reigning as the world's most formidable point scorer. Paris was his crowning achievement.

With the gold medal around his neck, Ikezaki embraced his wife and children and wept. "Rugby is a combat sport. The impact that smashes a wheelchair to pieces is proof that I'm alive"——those words are an anthem for everyone who battles illness.

A fading body and a blazing soul. Daisuke Ikezaki's story is proof of how far the human spirit can transcend the limits of the flesh. With every wheelchair he destroys, he etches another mark of "proof that he is alive."
