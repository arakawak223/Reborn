The Comeback Story

The comeback of Ayumu Hirano, told in 5 chapters

01

Origin–2014

The Boy Who Walks Through Dreams

Murakami City, Niigata Prefecture. His father, Hidetoshi, built a skate park by hand behind their home, and young Ayumu grew up there. "Walking through dreams" — true to the name his mother gave him, he started snowboarding at age four and his talent blossomed in the blink of an eye.

At 14, he competed at the Sochi Olympics and won a silver medal. As the youngest medalist at the time, he stunned the world. With both his older brother and younger brother also professional snowboarders, he was the very embodiment of a snowboarding family.

02

Despair2017

Liver Damage — The First Encounter with Death

In 2017, a fall during practice damaged his liver. At the same time, he tore a ligament in his left knee. Facing internal bleeding — an injury that threatened his life — the 19-year-old became conscious of death for the first time.

"When I injured my liver, I thought about death for the first time. But that experience stripped away all the noise in my mind. 'I'll ride so I have no regrets, no matter when it ends.' The strength I have now began with that despair."

In the year leading up to the PyeongChang Olympics, he had to rebuild not just his body, but his mind as well.

03

The Void2018–2021

Living with Fear

After returning from injury, Ayumu found he could no longer perform the same tricks as before. It wasn't a physical problem — it was fear.

"Fear doesn't go away. But that fear makes you more careful, and ultimately stronger."

He learned to embrace fear not as an enemy, but as an ally. Delicate landings, millimeter-level adjustments — fear sharpened his technique to an even finer edge.

During this period, he also took on skateboarding and qualified for the Tokyo Olympics. Competing in both summer and winter Games reflected his way of life — never depending on a single place of belonging.

04

AwakeningFebruary 2022

The "Triple Cork 1440" Etched into the Beijing Sky

The halfpipe final at the 2022 Beijing Olympics. Ayumu landed the world's first "Triple Cork 1440" on the biggest stage — a trick that defies physics, rotating three times while flipping four times.

Despite executing it flawlessly on his second run, the judges' score was controversially low. But Ayumu channeled his anger into power, and on his third run, he threw down the Triple Cork 1440 once again. The result: an undeniable gold medal.

"I didn't ride out of anger. I rode to prove who I am."

05

Rebirth2026–

A Fractured Pelvis, and On to Milan

In January 2026, he fractured his pelvis (ilium) during practice. A devastating injury with the Milan Olympics just around the corner. Yet Ayumu chose not to withdraw.

"It would be a lie to say it doesn't hurt. But I don't have time to wait for the bone to heal. I ride with the feeling of controlling my body through sheer will. The fracture is part of me."

Liver damage, a torn knee ligament, and now a fractured pelvis — no matter how many times he breaks, he rises again. The boy who "walks through dreams" is still living true to his name at 27.

For the sake of his first child, born in 2025, he continues to ride.
