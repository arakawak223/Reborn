The Comeback Story

The comeback of Uchu Tomita, told in 5 chapters

01

Origin–Early 2010s

The Boy Who Swam in the Light

Born in Kumamoto City, Kumamoto Prefecture, Uchu — whose name means "universe" — was a boy who seemed to carry infinite possibility within him, just as his grand name suggested. He took to swimming from a young age, making his mark in the world of competitive swimming as an able-bodied athlete. Swimming fast was his reason for living, and time spent in the water was his greatest happiness.

During high school, while devoting himself to swimming, he noticed something strange. The letters on the blackboard were becoming hard to read. Walking at night frightened him. The diagnosis was retinitis pigmentosa — a progressive visual impairment. His field of vision would gradually narrow, and eventually all light would be lost. A teenager was forced to absorb this devastating prognosis.

Yet Uchu never stopped swimming. Even as he battled the terror of his fading eyesight, he knew that in the water, he could be free. While the light remained, he swam with everything he had.

02

Despair2010s

Total Darkness — The Day He Lost the Light

The deterioration of his vision was relentless. His field of view shrank inexorably until, at last, the light was gone completely. It was a gradual despair. What he could see today might be invisible tomorrow. Living with that fear day after day was beyond imagination.

The dream of competing at the top as an able-bodied swimmer was quietly, but irreversibly, extinguished. After graduating from university, Uchu took a job at a private company. He left swimming behind. "There's no point in swimming anymore" — there was a time when he truly believed that.

In the darkness, he felt the sensation of freedom he had once known in the water slipping away into the deepest recesses of his memory. In a world he could not see, he began to lose sight of who he even was.

03

The VoidLate 2010s

The Voice of the Water, Found in Darkness

Even as he worked at his company, there was a hollow emptiness inside Uchu. Something was missing. He knew that "something" was swimming. He knew it, but he was afraid to admit it.

The turning point came when he learned about para swimming. There were athletes with visual impairments competing for the top of the world. There were people swimming on, in the same sightless world he inhabited.

"Because I lost my sight, the resistance of the water and the movements of my muscles became more vivid than vision ever was." When Uchu returned to the pool, he heard the "voice" of the water — something he had never noticed before. Water does not lie. The shifts in current, the subtle differences in water pressure, the movement of every individual muscle fiber — losing his sight had made all of these astonishingly clear.

The light he found in his struggle through darkness was waiting for him in the water. Uchu returned to competition.

04

AwakeningSummer 2021

Chasing Kimura Keiichi

When Uchu returned to para swimming, one rival stood in his way: Keiichi Kimura. Kimura had been completely blind since age two — a congenitally visually impaired athlete and the undisputed king of para swimming. His background was entirely different from Uchu, who had lost his sight later in life.

"By chasing Kimura, I stopped fearing the darkness." Through pursuing Kimura, Uchu honed his own swimming. Their relationship was that of rivals, close friends, and comrades-in-arms. Swimming side by side, sensing each other's presence in the dark — it was a bond beyond what any sighted person could imagine.

At the 2021 Tokyo Paralympics, Uchu won silver and bronze medals. And when Kimura claimed gold, it was Uchu in the adjacent lane who delivered the result. "Gold!" — that single word contained everything between them.

With his analytical mind and the fighting spirit sparked by Kimura's presence, Uchu's swimming grew sharper with every competition.

05

Rebirth2024–

A New Self Reflected in the Waters of Paris

At the 2024 Paris Paralympics, Uchu once again won a silver medal. A man who had lost all light, yet continued to rank among the top three in the world.

"Despair is nothing more than a screen onto which a new self is projected." This statement is the story of his life. On the screen of despair where his dreams as an able-bodied swimmer were extinguished, what appeared was a vision of himself shining in the new world of para swimming.

Just as his name — Uchu, "universe" — symbolizes, his potential is infinite. Even having lost the light, the world he sees in the water continues to expand. The story of a man who left his company, feared the darkness, and yet plunged back into the water teaches us that "seeing" is not the entirety of the world.

Uchu swims on through the darkness. Nothing is reflected in his eyes. But in his heart, a landscape more vivid than anyone else's stretches out before him.
