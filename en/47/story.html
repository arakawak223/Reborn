The Comeback Story

The comeback of Ryoyu Kobayashi, told in 5 chapters

01

Origin–2017

The Ski-Jumping Family from Iwate

Hachimantai, Iwate Prefecture. Born the youngest of four brothers, Ryoyu Kobayashi grew up in the extraordinary household where every sibling was a ski jumper. The rich natural beauty and harsh winters of Iwate nurtured the boy's instinct for flight.

His idol was the legend of Japanese ski jumping, Noriaki Kasai. After graduating from high school, he joined Kasai's team at Tsuchiya Home, learning "the art of flight" directly from the master.

"Whoever has the most fun wins. There's nothing better than flying through the sky"——a carefree smile and a singular sense of floating in midair. Ryoyu Kobayashi's jumps captivated everyone who watched. But the path to the top of the world would prove steeper than he ever anticipated.

02

Despair2017–2018

"Talented, but Mentally Fragile"

In his early years, Kobayashi was plagued by a knee injury. For a jumper, the knee is the linchpin of the takeoff, and pain directly undermines performance.

But what tortured him more than any physical ailment was a mental slump. Results on the World Cup circuit eluded him, and those around him labeled him "talented, but mentally fragile."

In ski jumping, where wind plays an outsized role, factors beyond an athlete's control can determine the outcome. A headwind that stunts distance, a plummeting ranking. The bitterness of a result that cannot be dismissed as "bad luck" gnawed at his young heart.

There were times when his confidence nearly collapsed. The question "Can I really compete on the world stage?" flickered through his mind.

03

The Void2018–2021

Kasai's Example and the Patience to Wait for the Wind

What rescued Kobayashi from his slump was the example of his mentor, Noriaki Kasai. The sight of the legend still competing past the age of 50 was a living symbol of "the power of persistence."

"Because I went through that terrible slump, I learned the patience to wait for the wind."

What he absorbed from Kasai was not technique alone. It was the relentless building of foundational fitness, the mental fortitude to believe in oneself, and "the heart that refuses to rot when results don't come." The legend told him: "Ryoyu, you have the talent to fly. The only thing left is your mind."

Using video games to unwind, he gradually mastered the art of mental equilibrium. The switch between on and off. Total focus on the jump hill; all smiles the moment he came down. That natural, unforced style revived his innate "ability to enjoy."

Conversations with his brothers also sustained him. The reassurance of having family in the same sport. Companions with whom he could share his worries and share a laugh were right beside him.

04

AwakeningFebruary 2022

The Beijing Olympics——Japan's First Individual Gold in 24 Years

The 2022 Beijing Olympics. Ryoyu Kobayashi delivered Japan's first individual ski jumping gold medal in 24 years——the first since Kazuyoshi Funaki at the 1998 Nagano Games.

Gold on the normal hill, silver on the large hill. On the face of the double medalist, there was no trace of the slump that once haunted him. Standing on the podium with that carefree grin, he embodied his own philosophy: "Whoever has the most fun wins."

"He is a modern genius. He fused the mental toughness inherited from Kasai with his own unique sense of flight."——The assessment from the jumping community was the crystallization of a master-pupil bond.

Having overcome a knee injury and a mental slump, having learned the patience to wait for the wind, the boy finally stood at the pinnacle of the world.

05

Rebirth2022–

The Genius Who Keeps Flying

Even after the Beijing Olympics, Ryoyu Kobayashi has continued to claim World Cup overall titles, leading the global ski jumping circuit. His goal is to enjoy a long career, much like his mentor Kasai.

"Whoever has the most fun wins"——that philosophy has proven the most powerful shortcut to victory. It is precisely because he once nearly buckled under pressure that he now possesses the "ability to enjoy."

The youngest of four brothers, raised among the snowy mountains of Iwate, found a mentor in Kasai, overcame mental fragility, and traced a golden arc across the Beijing sky. Ski jumping is a sport governed by the unpredictable variable of wind. But Kobayashi knows: whether the wind blows from behind or in his face, as long as he enjoys the act of flying, the wind will eventually become his ally.

The genius of the sky steps onto the jump hill today with a smile. The story of the man who filled a 24-year void is still being written.
