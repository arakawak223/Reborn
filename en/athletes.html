RE:BORN — The Comeback Athlete Archive

Stories of athletes who overcame devastating injuries and made remarkable comebacks

Athletes

Featured Athletes

01

Mark McMorrisマーク・マクモリス

"Just being able to ride makes me happy. Medals are just a bonus."

Snowboard / Canada

In 2017, he crashed into a tree after a failed backcountry jump landing in Whistler. He suffered 17 fractures (jaw, left arm, pelvis, ribs, etc.), a collapsed lung, and a ruptured spleen, leaving him in a coma.

02

Ayumu Hirano平野 歩夢

"I walk through my dreams. The name my mother gave me became the way I live."

Snowboard / Japan

In March 2017, he struck the deck of a halfpipe and ruptured his liver. In January 2026, he fractured his pelvis (ilium) just before the Milan Olympics.

03

Keiji Okamoto岡本 圭司

"Rather than counting what I've lost, it's more fun to think about what I can do with what I still have."

Para Snowboard / Japan

In 2015, he fell from a cliff during a filming session. He slammed into concrete, suffering 15 fractures across his body, spinal cord damage, and paralysis below the right knee.

04

Shohei Ohtani大谷 翔平

"I see rehab as time to upgrade myself."

Baseball / Japan

Torn UCL (ulnar collateral ligament) in his right elbow. Underwent surgery twice — in 2018 and 2023 (Tommy John surgery, among others).

05

Andy Murrayアンディ・マレー

"Being able to play the sport I love without pain — that alone makes me a winner."

Tennis / United Kingdom

Osteoarthritis of the hip. He endured years of excruciating pain so severe that even putting on socks was a struggle.

06

Shingo Kunieda国枝 慎吾

"I am the strongest!"

Wheelchair Tennis / Japan

Spinal cord tumor at age 9. In 2016, severe pain from loose cartilage fragments (joint mice) in his right elbow.

07

Rikako Ikee池江 璃花子

"Adversity doesn't visit those who can't overcome it. God only gives trials to those strong enough to endure them."

Swimming / Japan

Diagnosed with acute lymphoblastic leukemia in 2019.

08

Tiger Woodsタイガー・ウッズ

"I wanted to play with my son again. That wish is what got me back on my feet."

Golf / United States

In 2021, a car accident left him with a shattered right leg and the threat of amputation.

09

Kaoru Mitoma三笘 薫

"I saw my injury as a gift — a chance to objectively analyze my own play."

Soccer/Football / Japan

In 2024, suffered a lumbar spine injury.

10

Hifumi Abe阿部 一二三

"Having the courage to take a step back gives you the strength to take two steps forward."

Judo / Japan

Finger injuries and the intense pressure of national team selection.

11

Keiichi Kimura木村 敬一

"I want to measure happiness by my own yardstick, not someone else's."

Para Swimming / Japan

Non-congenital blindness (lost sight at age 2). The psychological defeat of winning four medals at Rio 2016 but none of them gold.

12

Abdul Hakim Sani Brownサニブラウン・アブデル・ハキーム

"The time I spent injured was a research period — a dialogue with my own body."

Track & Field / Japan

2020–21: lumbar spondylolysis and hamstring injuries.

13

Mima Ito伊藤 美誠

"The frustration of losing becomes the greatest investment for winning the next match."

Table Tennis / Japan

Lower back pain, and the psychological blow of failing to qualify for the 2024 Paris Olympics team.

14

Mao Toratani虎谷 真央

"When I hold a sword, it doesn't matter that my legs don't work. In that world, I'm free."

Wheelchair Fencing / Japan

Spinal cord injury from an accident.

15

Kevin Durantケビン・デュラント

"Hard work beats talent when talent doesn't work hard."

Basketball / United States

During the 2019 NBA Finals, he ruptured his right Achilles tendon — an injury often considered a career death sentence for basketball players.

16

Ran Takahashi高橋 藍

"The time I can't stand on the court is time to see volleyball more objectively than anyone else."

Volleyball / Japan

In 2024, suffered an ankle injury during a critical league stretch. A major source of anxiety heading into the Olympics.

17

Michael Leitchリーチ マイケル

"A crisis is a chance to test our bond. If you don't run away, a path will always open."

Rugby / Japan (born in New Zealand)

Just before the 2019 World Cup, he suffered hip and knee injuries. He could barely walk as the tournament approached.

18

Sena Irie入江 聖奈

"I have no natural talent. That's exactly why the only way I can feel secure is by training harder than everyone else."

Boxing / Japan

A deep slump during the national team selection period. A string of losses drove her to the brink of retirement — a profound psychological crisis.

19

Noriaki Kasai葛西 紀明

"Hard work never betrays you. If you feel betrayed, you simply haven't worked hard enough."

Ski Jumping / Japan

A major knee injury in his early career. Years of poor results followed. Throughout his 30s, he was widely dismissed as a 'washed-up' athlete.

20

Masahiro Tanaka田中 将大

"When it comes to obsessing over every single pitch, I refuse to lose to anyone."

Baseball / Japan

In 2014, a partial tear of his right elbow ligament. A condition that would normally require Tommy John surgery.

21

Yuki Ishikawa石川 祐希

"If you want to be the best in the world, you need to put yourself in the toughest environment in the world."

Volleyball / Japan

In 2023, worsening back pain and lower-body injuries during the season. The pressure of being national team captain compounded the situation.

22

Yoshinobu Yamamoto山本 由伸

"True strength comes from questioning conventional wisdom."

Baseball / Japan

In 2024, during his first year with the Dodgers, he was sidelined long-term with a right shoulder injury (triceps strain).

23

Ai Fukuhara福原 愛

"Even if you lose, if you take something from it, it's not really a loss."

Table Tennis / Japan

In 2014, fractured her left big toe. Additionally, years of chronic pain in her right elbow and right shoulder.

24

Naoya Inoue井上 尚弥

"Only an overwhelming volume of training can erase fear inside the ring."

Boxing / Japan

During his 2019 fight against Nonito Donaire, he suffered an orbital floor fracture and broken nose. He was seeing double in the middle of the bout — a desperate situation.

25

Shingo Kunieda国枝 慎吾

"My wheelchair is my 'legs.' How I command it defines my tennis."

Wheelchair Tennis / Japan

Right elbow surgery just before the 2016 Rio Paralympics. Afterward, he fell into a near-yips state from pain, and his world ranking plummeted.

26

Mami Tani谷 真海

"Instead of counting what I've lost, I want to maximize the possibilities that remain."

Para Triathlon / Para Athletics / Japan

In 2002, her right foot was amputated below the ankle due to osteosarcoma. Then in 2011, the Great East Japan Earthquake devastated her family home.

27

Choji Murata村田 兆治

"Life is a complete game. I want to stay on my own two feet until the very end."

Baseball / Japan

In 1982, a devastating injury to his right elbow. At the time, the conventional wisdom was that surgery meant the end of a career.

28

Yuta Watanabe渡邊 雄太

"If you don't have talent, you have to be the first one in the gym and the last one to leave."

Basketball / Japan

Repeated hamstring and ankle injuries throughout his NBA career. The constant struggle of maintaining peak condition amid fierce competition.

29

Shogo Akiyama秋山 翔吾

"Preparation is everything. Results are just the answer sheet of your preparation."

Baseball / Japan

Foot injuries and a drastic loss of playing time during his MLB tenure. In 2022, he was effectively released by the Padres.

30

Kohei Uchimura内村 航平

"I'm no genius. I just practiced more and thought about gymnastics more than anyone else."

Gymnastics / Japan

Chronic injuries to both shoulders and torn ankle ligaments. By the time of the Tokyo Olympics, he was so worn down that he had to abandon the all-around and focus solely on the horizontal bar.

31

Uchu Tomita富田 宇宙

"Despair is nothing more than a screen onto which a new version of yourself is projected."

Para Swimming / Japan

A progressive visual impairment that eventually took all his sight. The devastating loss of his dream of competing at the highest level as an able-bodied athlete.

32

Mami Sato佐藤 真海

"Sport saved my life. That's why I want to give back through sport."

Para Athletics (Long Jump) / Japan

Right foot amputated due to osteosarcoma. Compounded by the 2011 Great East Japan Earthquake, which destroyed her family home and took the lives of friends.

33

Alex Zanardiアレックス・ザナルディ

"When I lost both legs, I thought, 'Great — now I get to find a new hobby: handcycling.'"

Motorsport / Handcycling / Italy

In 2001, he collided at 200 mph during a race in Germany. Both legs were amputated, and he lost 70% of his blood — he was on the brink of death.

34

Christian Eriksenクリスチャン・エリクセン

"Just being able to step onto the pitch makes me feel alive."

Soccer/Football / Denmark

During Euro 2020 in 2021, he suffered cardiac arrest and collapsed on the pitch. His life hung in the balance.

35

Yasuaki Yamasaki山崎 康晃

"The mound is where I belong. I refuse to give it up to anyone."

Baseball / Japan

A prolonged slump around 2020. Hitters began reading his signature two-seam fastball, and he was demoted to the minors. Cruel voices declared him 'finished.'

36

Yuzuru Hanyu羽生 結弦

"Hard work can lie to you. But those lies are never wasted."

Figure Skating / Japan

Countless ligament injuries to his right ankle. Before the PyeongChang Olympics, a major injury kept him off the ice for months.

37

Wataru Endo遠藤 航

"Preparation is everything. The match is decided before it even starts."

Soccer/Football / Japan

A concussion just before the 2022 World Cup that put his participation in doubt. Additionally, an extended period on the bench at his club.

38

Yukiko Ueno上野 由岐子

"Limits are something you set for yourself. I can still evolve."

Softball / Japan

In 2019, a line drive struck her face during a game, causing a complex jaw fracture. A career-threatening injury.

39

Kei Nishikori錦織 圭

"Every time I come back from an injury, I rediscover the joy of playing tennis."

Tennis / Japan

Wrist, hip, ankle, elbow — a career-long battle with injuries throughout his entire body. Extended absence from 2022 to 2023.

40

Noriko Fujiwara藤原 紀子

"My wheelchair is my 'cool fighter jet.'"

Wheelchair Basketball / Japan

Lower-body paralysis caused by a traffic accident. The day her dream of walking on her own vanished.

41

Roki Sasaki佐々木 朗希

"Baseball is how I repay the people who supported me."

Baseball / Japan

Repeated setbacks due to the fragility of growing bones and recurring tightness in his right arm.

42

Saori Kimura木村 沙織

"Volleyball is about connecting everyone together. I got stronger the moment I realized it wasn't all on my shoulders."

Volleyball / Japan

Years of knee and back pain, fractured fingers. Compounded by the mental exhaustion of serving as team captain.

43

Naoko Takahashi高橋 尚子

"Enjoying what you do is always the shortest path to success."

Marathon / Japan

Plantar fasciitis and knee injuries after the Olympics. Every step sent searing pain through her body.

44

Hikaru Oe大江 光

"The fear of riding was nothing compared to the loneliness of not being able to ride at all."

Snowboard / Japan

Repeated ACL tears in both knees — the worst possible injury for a snowboarder.

45

Reia Taniguchi谷口 令亜

"My disability is just one part of who I am. On the board, we're all the same."

Para Snowboard / Japan

A congenital functional impairment in the left foot. Additionally, a major injury sustained during competition.

46

Daisuke Ikezaki池崎 大輔

"Rugby is a combat sport. The impact that shatters a wheelchair is proof that I'm alive."

Wheelchair Rugby / Japan

A progressive neuromuscular disease. Living with the constant fear of losing more grip strength and leg sensation year by year. Additional injuries from brutal on-court collisions.

47

Ryoyu Kobayashi小林 陵侑

"Have fun and you win. There's nothing better than flying through the air."

Ski Jumping / Japan

Knee injuries in his early career and a psychological slump when results stagnated. He was dogged by the label: 'talented but mentally fragile.'

48

Takayuki Suzuki鈴木 孝幸

"Rather than searching for what I'm missing, I focus on how to use what I have."

Para Swimming / Japan

Limb deficiencies in all four extremities. Compounded by years of shoulder strain from competition and the frustration of a 13-year gold medal drought.

49

Hideji Otaki大滝 秀治

"One step at a time — even if you have to crawl, that is the true path."

Kyudo / Theater (Spiritual Athlete) / Japan

Wartime conscription followed by postwar famine and lung disease. Years of obscurity as a struggling artist waiting for his breakthrough.
