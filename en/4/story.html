The Comeback Story

The comeback of Shohei Ohtani, told in 5 chapters

01

Origin–2018

A Baseball Boy from Iwate and the Dream Mapped on a Mandala Chart

Oshu City, Iwate Prefecture. Born to a father who played corporate baseball and a mother who was a badminton player, Shohei displayed extraordinary physical ability from an early age. At Hanamaki Higashi High School, he blossomed as a two-way player — both pitcher and hitter — and clocked 160 km/h.

On a mandala chart he created in junior high, he wrote his goal: "First-round draft pick, eight teams." He was a genius who visualized his dreams and systematically made them reality. In 2018, he headed for Major League Baseball. The moment he put on the Angels uniform, a Japanese boy's dream stood on American soil.

02

Despair2018 & 2023

A Torn Right Elbow — The Two-Way Crisis

During the 2018 season, damage to the ulnar collateral ligament in his right elbow was diagnosed. He had no choice but to undergo Tommy John surgery. The elbow is a pitcher's most vital asset. At least eighteen months until recovery. The reality of "being unable to throw" shook the very foundation of his identity as a two-way player.

In 2023, he injured the same right elbow again. A second surgery. "The two-way experiment is over" — the verdict echoed from every corner of the world.

03

The Void2019 & 2024

Time to Update

"I think of rehabilitation as time to 'update' myself."

Ohtani reframed the period when he couldn't pitch as "an opportunity to focus entirely on hitting." He studied motion-analysis data exhaustively, built up his strength, and optimized his batting mechanics. Not a single second of his rehab went to waste.

"Because I was injured, I was able to think about how to use my body more efficiently than before" — this ability to convert despair into "time for evolution" was Shohei Ohtani's greatest weapon.

04

Awakening2024

50-50 — The Day History Moved

In 2024, having moved to the Dodgers, Ohtani achieved the first-ever "50 home runs and 50 stolen bases" season in MLB history during a year spent solely as a hitter. All the frustration of being unable to pitch was poured into his bat and his baserunning.

He reached 51-51 on the final game of the season. The stadium erupted, and even the opposing team's bench broke into applause. A season lost to injury on the mound became a season that rewrote the record books at the plate.

05

Rebirth2025–

The Day He Throws Again — The Return of the Complete Player

In 2025, he returned as a pitcher. Having overcome two elbow surgeries, the "complete Shohei Ohtani" — dominating as both pitcher and hitter over a full season — was back.

"He doesn't waste a single second, even during rehab. That focus is his true talent." (Manager Dave Roberts)

Two surgeries on his right elbow. Yet he never abandoned the two-way dream. Every time he was injured, he came back as an updated version of himself. Shohei Ohtani's return deserves to be called not a "recovery" but an "evolution."
