The Comeback Story

The comeback of Yuta Watanabe, told in 5 chapters

01

Origin–2018

A Basketball Family's Resolve -- Chasing the NBA

Miki, Kagawa Prefecture. Yuta Watanabe grew up in a household where both his father and mother were former basketball players; he lived with a ball in his hands from the time he was small. Yet what he possessed was not "talent" but "resolve."

Japanese basketball was far removed from the world stage in those days. Still, Watanabe committed himself: "I will become a player who can compete globally." After graduating high school he enrolled at an American university. The language barrier, cultural differences, the overwhelming physical gap -- everything was a wall.

He spent four years at George Washington University, absorbing American basketball firsthand. The dream of standing on an NBA court was still a long way off. But he knew: if talent was not his gift, he had no choice but to outwork everyone.

"If you don't have talent, you come to the gym earlier than anyone and leave later than anyone" -- he set about living those words every single day.

02

Despair2018–2020

Recurring Injuries and the Tightrope of a Two-Way Contract

The NBA proved even harsher than he had imagined. To survive as a Japanese player with inferior physical tools, Watanabe threw his body on the line every game. The price was a recurring hamstring injury. Ankle sprains became frequent as well.

A two-way contract -- shuttling between the NBA and the G League (the developmental league) in a precarious position. He could be waived at any moment, and in that context, an injury was essentially fatal. Get hurt and you can't play. Can't play and you can't prove yourself. Can't prove yourself and the contract ends.

During rehabilitation, watching teammates thrive was the hardest part. "The team goes on just fine without me" -- that reality. Days when his very identity as an NBA player wavered.

"The time you can't be on the court because of injury is the most important time to face your inner self" -- he told himself that, yet the anxiety would not fade.

03

The Void2020–2021

Sharpening His Weapons Off the Court

Watanabe refused to let the time lost to injury go to waste. Alongside rehabilitation, he devoted himself to improving his three-point shooting accuracy.

To survive in the NBA, he needed a clear "weapon." If he could not win with physicality, he would win with shooting precision. He would win with defensive instincts. He would become indispensable to his team through sheer dedication -- this was the survival strategy Watanabe devised.

Hundreds of shots, then thousands. He resumed training the moment each healed body part allowed it, gradually refining his form. It was painstaking, unglamorous work. But for Watanabe, it was "the only weapon available to someone without talent."

His wife, Akiko Kuji, was his emotional pillar. She supported her husband as he fought in a foreign land with an unwavering smile. Words from a mentor also sustained him: "Your effort will be rewarded" -- trusting in those words, Watanabe pressed on through the darkness.

04

Awakening2021–2023

A Standard Contract -- Earning a Place in the NBA

Overcoming recurring injuries and the instability of a two-way contract, Yuta Watanabe finally earned a standard NBA contract. Stints with the Raptors and then the Nets. His three-point accuracy and defensive commitment earned recognition, and he became an integral part of his team.

"If you don't have talent, you come to the gym earlier than anyone and leave later than anyone."

Days lived exactly by those words bore fruit at last. The reason NBA stars respected Watanabe was not talent; it was the density of his effort.

"I've never seen anyone work as hard as he does. Even the star players respect him." (Former NBA teammate)

The second Japanese-born player in NBA history. Behind that title lay countless injuries, lonely nights, and a passion that never died.

05

Rebirth2023–

A Historic World Cup Run, and the Future of Basketball

The 2023 FIBA World Cup. Yuta Watanabe, as a core member of the Japanese national team, contributed to a historic victory. The experience and skills honed in the NBA became the nucleus of the national squad.

Six seasons of NBA play. For Japanese basketball, Watanabe's existence was "proof of hope." He proved that even without innate talent, effort and dedication could earn a place on the NBA stage.

Time and again injuries struck, and each time Watanabe returned with a new weapon in hand. His story is not a "tale of talent" but a "tale of effort."

"The time you can't be on the court because of injury is the most important time to face your inner self" -- the weapons he forged off the court shone on the grandest stage in the world. A boy raised in a basketball family, armed with nothing but resolve, changed the history of Japanese basketball.
