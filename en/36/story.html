The Comeback Story

The comeback of Yuzuru Hanyu, told in 5 chapters

01

Origin–2014

The Genius Who Descended onto the Ice of Sendai

Sendai, Miyagi Prefecture. Yuzuru began figure skating at age four, and from his earliest days he was a prodigy on the ice. The asthmatic boy took up skating because of his older sister's influence. He had heard that the cold air of the rink was good for asthma — but that chance encounter would change the world.

March 11, 2011. The Great East Japan Earthquake struck Sendai. Yuzuru was near his home when the disaster hit, and he spent four days living in an evacuation shelter. His home rink was damaged, and he lost his place to train. "Maybe I should quit skating" — there was a time when that thought crossed his mind.

But he continued competing, practicing at rinks across the country that opened their doors to him. The boy who engraved in his heart the preciousness of being able to skate at all went on to win gold at the 2014 Sochi Olympics at the age of nineteen. All of Japan erupted in celebration.

02

Despair2014–2017

Countless Screams from His Right Ankle

The gold medalist's right ankle screamed in pain again and again. During a practice collision at the 2014 Cup of China, he tore the ligaments. After that, he suffered repeated ligament injuries to the same right ankle — so many that the count became impossible to keep.

In November 2017, during official practice for the NHK Trophy, he fell while attempting a quadruple Lutz. Once again, the ligaments in his right ankle were torn. This time, it was severe. The Pyeongchang Olympics were just three months away. Yet he could not even stand on the ice.

"There's no way he can make it in time." Everyone around him thought so. The dream of back-to-back Olympic gold was sinking into the searing pain of his right ankle. Days where he could not function without painkillers. The ankle of the world's greatest skater had long since exceeded its limits.

03

The VoidWinter 2017

The Seeker on Ice Who Taught Himself Anatomy

Days when he could not set foot on the ice. But Yuzuru did not stop. While taking excessive doses of painkillers, he began an exhaustive study of his own body. He devoured anatomy textbooks, seeking to understand the structure of his right ankle down to the millimeter.

"The worse the pain in my right ankle got, the more I was able to set myself free." Behind this paradoxical statement lay Yuzuru's scientific approach to coexisting with pain. At what angle should he land to minimize the load on his ankle? At what moment should he shift his weight to avoid the pain?

He became the engineer of his own body. Relying not on sensation alone, but on data and anatomical knowledge, he devised methods to control the pain. His trusting relationship with coach Brian Orser sustained him through the lonely battle. And the support of fans from around the world held together a spirit on the verge of breaking.

"Hard work can betray you. But that betrayal is never wasted." Days of training that bore no fruit. And yet Yuzuru continued to sharpen both body and mind, believing in the day he would return to the ice.

04

AwakeningFebruary 2018

Gold at Pyeongchang — From Injury to Instant Legend

February 2018. The men's singles figure skating event at the Pyeongchang Olympics. Roughly three months since his major right ankle injury. He was competing virtually cold, with almost no preparation.

In both practice and competition, there was no trace of fear on Yuzuru's face. He took the lead with his short program, "Ballade No. 1," then nailed one quadruple jump after another in his free skate, "SEIMEI." With each landing, pain shot through his right ankle — pain he minimized through precise weight control. The fruits of those days teaching himself anatomy were distilled into every single jump.

The result: back-to-back Olympic gold. The first man to defend the Olympic title in men's figure skating in sixty-six years. An injured skater winning Olympic gold — this was not a "comeback." It was a legend.

With the gold medal in his hands, Yuzuru dedicated it to the people of Sendai, and to everyone who had been wounded by the earthquake.

05

Rebirth2022–

Turning Professional — The Eternal Seeker on Ice

At the 2022 Beijing Olympics, he attempted the unprecedented quadruple Axel. He fell, but the first-ever attempt by a human being was etched into history. He finished fourth. But Yuzuru was at peace. "I gave everything I had."

That July, he announced his retirement from competition and his transition to a professional career. The man who had achieved the "Super Slam" — winning the Olympics, the World Championships, the Grand Prix Final, and the Four Continents Championships — was now free from the constraints of scoring.

"He is beloved by the gods of ice, but more than that, he is a demon who pushes himself beyond all limits." That assessment from the international media rings true. What drove Yuzuru was never talent alone — it was an insatiable thirst for knowledge and a spirit that could turn even pain into an ally.

As a professional skater, he performs solo ice shows before sold-out audiences, skating freely. His right ankle is still not fully healed. But pain is part of who he is. The boy from Sendai who lived through an earthquake, overcame injury after injury, and won back-to-back Olympic gold now skates more freely than anyone on ice unmarked by judges' scores.

Yuzuru Hanyu's story is not one of "recovery" but of "transcendence." Pain, fear, all of it — transformed into part of the performance. He is the eternal seeker on ice. His journey has no end.
